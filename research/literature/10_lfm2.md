# LFM2 Technical Report

- **Paper:** [arXiv:2511.23404](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.23404)
- **Version reviewed:** v1, 28 November 2025
- **Primary topic:** hardware-in-the-loop edge architecture search

## Central claim

For latency-sensitive CPU and edge deployment, Liquid AI's architecture search selects a surprisingly
minimal hybrid: cheap gated short-convolution blocks in most layers and a small number of GQA blocks.
Adding a linear-attention or SSM branch to the local block does not improve the measured
quality/latency/memory Pareto frontier under the search budget.

This is the closest published relative of Speck's original gated-convolution path and an important
counterweight to the assumption that every non-attention layer needs a large recurrent state.

## Architecture

The convolution block uses two input-dependent gates around a depthwise kernel-3 convolution:

`(B, C, h_tilde) = Linear(h)`, `y = B * h_tilde`, `z = DepthwiseConv3(y)`,
`out = LinearOut(C * z)`.

Thus the block has local receptive field only; all true long-range token interaction must flow through
the interleaved GQA layers and depth.

| LFM2-350M/700M/1.2B | 16 | 6 | 1,024/1,536/2,048 | 32K | dense |
| LFM2-2.6B | 30 | 8 | 2,048 | 32K | dense |
| LFM2-8B-A1B | 24 | 6 | 2,048 | 32K | 8.3B/1.5B |

The common shorthand “10 convolution + 6 attention” applies to the 16-layer dense models. The larger
models use different counts. Attention has QK-Norm, RoPE, eight KV groups, and 64-dimensional heads.
Every layer also has a SwiGLU MLP. Despite its product name, LFM2-8B-A1B reports **1.5B active
parameters**, not exactly 1B; its MoE layers use 32 experts with top-4 routing, while the first two layers
remain dense.

## Hardware-in-the-loop search

Candidates are trained on a reference dataset, then filtered on quality, time to first token, p50/p95
decode latency, prefill throughput, and peak resident memory at 4K/32K. Measurements use batch 1 and
matched tokenizer, quantization, and backend settings on phone-class CPUs/NPUs.

The released CPU tables use `llama.cpp` Q4_0. Examples on a Snapdragon 8 Elite phone include:

- LFM2-1.2B at a 4K prompt: 222 prefill tokens/s and 55.5 decode tokens/s, versus Qwen3-1.7B at
  98 and 26.9;
- LFM2-2.6B: 116 prefill and 30.0 decode tokens/s, ahead of the listed dense 3B–4B baselines;
- results vary on the Ryzen laptop, where Gemma-3-1B is competitive in raw throughput.

These are device-specific Pareto results, not a universal `2×` speed constant.

## Training and quality

Dense checkpoints pretrain for about 10T tokens at 4K, then receive a 32K long-context mid-training
phase; the MoE uses a similar 12T-token recipe. The report's final evaluation includes substantial SFT,
preference alignment, curriculum ordering, and model merging. For example, LFM2-1.2B reports IFEval
`74.89` and LFM2-2.6B reports `79.56`, but those numbers do not isolate the backbone.

## What matters for Speck

LFM2 defines a strong edge baseline: local gated convolution plus only enough attention to recover global
capability. It should be compared with KDA/GDN on the actual target hardware, because a theoretically
more expressive recurrence can lose if its state update and kernels are memory-bound.

A fair Speck screen should measure:

- short and long validation loss at fixed tokens/FLOPs;
- high-load retrieval, which local convolution cannot solve alone;
- batch-1 TTFT and p50/p95 decode on target CPU and GPU;
- peak RSS including cache and temporary workspaces;
- quality after matching the number and placement of GQA layers.

## Limitations and cautions

- The architecture-search dataset, full candidate set, and every rejected result are not released in the
  paper, limiting independent reconstruction of the Pareto search.
- A 32K target can favor local convolution differently from a 128K–1M target.
- Strong final benchmark results mix architecture, 10T+ tokens, curated data, post-training, custom
  parsing, and merging.
- Six or eight GQA layers still create a linearly growing KV cache and quadratic global prefill.

## Bottom line

LFM2 is the “do we need a recurrent state at all?” control. For edge inference it shows that a very cheap
local block plus a minority of GQA can win the measured Pareto frontier. Speck should require KDA/GDN
to justify their extra state and kernel complexity on both capability and target-hardware metrics.
